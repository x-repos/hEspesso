Abstract

The  deposition  of  ultraﬁne  single-atom  nickel  particles  on  Nb2 C  (MXene)  was  successfully  achieved  using  a  wet  chemistry  method
to synthesize Ni@Nb2 C composite. This study explored the effect of Ni@Nb2 C on the hydrogen absorption and desorption properties of
MgH2 through theoretical calculations and experimental investigations. Under the catalytic action of Ni@Nb2 C, the initial dehydrogenation
temperature of MgH2  was reduced by 121 °C, with approximately 4.26 wt.% of H2  desorbed at 225 °C in 100 min. The dehydrogenation
activation energy of the MgH2  + Ni@Nb2 C composite dropped to 86.7 kJ ·mol−1 , a reduction of 60.5 kJ ·mol−1  compared to pure MgH2 .
Density functional theory calculations indicated that the incorporation of Ni@Nb2 C enhanced the performance of MgH2  performance by
improving interactions among Nb2 C, Ni, Mg, and H atoms. In the Ni@Nb2 C + MgH2 system, the lengths of Mg-H bonds (1.91–1.99 ˚A) were
found to be longer than those observed in pure MgH2 (1.71 ˚A). The dehydrogenation energy for this system (1.08 eV) was lower than that for
Nb2 C (1.52 eV). These ﬁndings suggest that the synergistic effect of Ni and Nb2 C signiﬁcantly enhances the hydrogenation/dehydrogenation
kinetics of MgH2 , thereby introducing a novel approach for catalytic modiﬁcation of solid hydrogen storage materials through synergistic
actions.

Catalyst  doping  is  a  convenient  and  effective  method  to
deal with the aforesaid problems of MgH2 [ 13–25 ]. Adding a
catalyst to magnesium-based hydrogen storage materials not
only  enhances  efﬁciency  but  also  offers  the  advantages  of
straightforward  operation.  Cui  et  al.  [ 16 ]  successfully  pre-
pared  core-shell-structured  Mg-TM  (TM  =  Ti,  Nb,  V,  Co,
Mo,  and  Ni)  nanocomposites  and  found  that  except  for  Ni,
the smaller the TM electronegativity, the stronger the positive
effect  of  TM  on  the  hydrogenation  reaction  of  MgH2 .  Dan
et al. [ 18 ] conducted in situ synthesis of Mg2 Ni/Mg2 NiH4 by
incorporating  Ni  into  Mg/MgH2  systems.  This  process  was
accompanied by volume expansion and the generation of mi-
croscopic strain, which could function as a hydrogen pump to
facilitate efﬁcient hydrogen storage and release. Liu et al. [ 25 ]
introduced K2 Ti6 O13  into MgH2 , resulting in a reduction of
the starting dehydrogenation temperature of the composite to
175 °C. The enhancement in the hydrogen storage properties
of  this  composite  was  attributed  to  the  synergistic  catalytic
effects among KMgH3 , TiO and Ti, which were derived from
K2 Ti6 O13 . MXenes represent a class of two-dimensional in-
organic compounds that have attracted considerable attention
in  recent  years.  These  materials  are  primarily  composed  of
transition  metal  carbides,  nitrides,  or  carbonatites  and  typi-
cally consist of multiple atomic layers. MXenes have exhib-
ited  promising  performance  in  the  domains  of  electrochem-
istry  and  energy  storage  applications  [ 10 , 26–31 ].  Pan  et  al.
[ 31 ]  synthesized  two-dimensional  Ti3 C2  through  a  stripping
method and identiﬁed that its unique layered structure, along
with the in-situ formation of metallic titanium, may be cru-
cial factors contributing to the high catalytic activity of Ti3 C2 .
This  phenomenon  was  supported  by  the  capability  of  dehy-
drogenated  MgH2  to  absorb  hydrogen  at  room  temperature,
signiﬁcantly  enhancing  its  hydrogen  storage  reaction  kinet-
ics. Similarly, NbTiC and Ti2 VC2 have signiﬁcantly enhanced
the hydrogen storage properties of MgH2 [ 32 , 33 ]. When inte-
grated with MgH2 , MXene materials with their unique layered
structure could facilitate rapid diffusion pathways for hydro-
gen molecules. Moreover, their large speciﬁc surface area typ-
ically  harbors  abundant  active  sites  that  serve  as  adsorption
and dissociation centers for hydrogen molecules [ 31 ].

In  recent  years,  research  has  demonstrated  that  employ-
ing  two  or  more  catalysts  to  create  composite  catalysts  can
signiﬁcantly  enhance  the  hydrogen  storage  performance  of
MgH2 . This improvement  is attributed to the synergistic ef-
fects arising from interactions among the components within
the composite catalyst [ 34–41 ]. Our previous studies [ 42–45 ]
and  theoretical  calculations  [ 46 ]  have  further  conﬁrmed  the
enhanced effect of synergistic catalysis through multiple cat-
alysts. In this study, we employed a novel and effective strat-
egy to anchor Ni nanoparticles onto the surface of Nb2 C via
a wet chemical method, resulting in a functionalized Nb2 C-
supported  Ni  composite.  The  hydrogen  storage  performance

2. Experimental details and computational modeling

2.1. Preparation of Ni@Nb2 C composite

The preparation process of the Ni@Nb2 C composite is il-
lustrated in Fig. 1 . A 2 g of Nb2 AlC (400mesh; 98%; Fos-
han Xinxi Technology Co., Ltd) was ﬁrst added to a Teﬂon
liner  containing  30  ml  of  hydroﬂuoric  acid  (HF;  49%;  Al-
addin)  and  reacted  at  50 °C  for  96  h.  The  resultant  product
was washed repeatedly by centrifugation with deionized wa-
ter  until  the  pH  value  of  the  supernatant  reached  above  6.
The  as-obtained  solid  product  was  dried  in  a  vacuum  oven
for 10 h to obtain multilayered Nb2 C. Subsequently, 0.1 g of
Nb2 C and 0.121 g of NiCl2 ·6H2 O (AR; 98%; Aladdin) were
ultrasonically dispersed in a mixed solution comprising 40 ml
of deionized (DI) water and ethylene glycol (C2 H6 O2 ; purity:
99.8%; Aladdin), maintaining a mass ratio of DI to C2 H6 O2
of 1:1. Additionally, 6 ml of hydrazine hydrate (N2 H4 ·H2 O;
50%;  Sinopharm)  and  4  ml  of  NaOH  (3  M;  Aladdin)  were
added  dropwise  into  the  as-prepared  mixed  solution  under
mechanical  stirring  at  a  speed  of  200  r/min.  The  reduction
reaction was carried out in a 50 °C oil bath for 6 h, and the
resultant  produce  was  washed  several  times  with  DI  water
through centrifugation. Finally, the black powder obtained af-
ter  freeze-drying  overnight  was  heat-treated  in  a  mixed  gas
atmosphere (Ar:H2 = 93:7). The powder was heated to 350 °C
at a rate of 5 °C ·min−1 and held at this temperature for 2 h to
remove residual organic matters on the surface. The furnace
was then cooled to room temperature to obtain the Ni@Nb2 C
composite. For comparison, Ni particles were produced under
the same experimental conditions.

2.2. Preparation of Ni@Nb2 C-doped, Ni-doped, and
Nb2 C-doped MgH2 samples

First, 0.075 g of Ni@Nb2 C and 0.925 g of MgH2  (98%;
Hebei  Langfang  Beide  Trading  Co.,  Ltd.)  were  ball-milled
for 5 h in a high-energy ball mill with a ball-to-material ratio
of  50:1  at  a  speed  of  500  rpm  to  prepare  Ni@Nb2 C-doped
MgH2 .  Similarly,  Ni-doped  MgH2  and  Nb2 C-doped  MgH2
were synthesized by uniformly mixing Ni and Nb2 C, respec-
tively, with MgH2 under the same conditions. To prevent ox-
idation,  the  aforementioned  processes  were  conducted  in  an
argon atmosphere.

2.4. Computational models

According  to  the  XRD  results,  Nb2 C  MXene  (space
group = P63 /mmc) was modeled using a 2 × 2 × 1 super-
cell with an 18 ˚A vacuum  between  the slabs. The structure
of  Ni@Nb2 C  was  built  by  adding  one  Ni  atom  on  each  C
atom  on  the  Nb2 C  surface,  and  the  MgH2  cluster  was  used
as the adsorbate. The MgH2 adsorption process on the surface
of Ni-adsorbed Nb2 C is displayed in Fig. 2 . All calculations
were executed in the Cambridge serial total energy package
[ 47 ]  with  the  Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof  exchange-correlation
functional of the generalized gradient approximation [ 48 , 49 ].

Ni@Nb2 C  +  MgH2  system,  H  atoms  accepted  most  of  the

interaction with the surface atoms.

4. Conclusions

The  negatively  charged  functional  groups  on  the  surface
of Nb2 C provided abundant attachment sites for Ni nanopar-
ticles,  and  Ni  particles  with  an  average  size  of  5  nm  were
loaded on the surface of Nb2 C to synthesize Ni@Nb2 C com-
posite. The Ni@Nb2 C + MgH2 system exhibited outstanding
hydrogen storage performance due to the synergistic catalytic
effect of Ni and Nb2 C on MgH2 . Dehydrogenated products,
such as Ni + MgH2 , Nb2 C + MgH2 , and Ni@Nb2 C + MgH2 ,
were  capable  of  absorbing  hydrogen  at  room  tempera-
ture;  however,  Ni@Nb2 C  +  MgH2  demonstrated  lower  de-
hydrogenation  temperatures  and  superior  hydrogen  absorp-
tion/desorption kinetics, indicating that Ni@Nb2 C has a more
pronounced impact on the hydrogen storage performance of
MgH2 . Furthermore, Ni@Nb2 C + MgH2 displayed excellent
cycling  stability  during  hydrogen  absorption/desorption  pro-
cesses.  The  ability  of  Nb2 C  to  reduce  the  adsorption  and
dissociation  energies  of  Mg/MgH2  facilitated  hydrogen  ab-
sorption  and  desorption  in  MgH2  +  Ni@Nb2 C,  causing  an
improvement in hydrogen storage performance. Additionally,
Ni and Mg atoms tended to form Mg2 Ni, which was subse-
quently transformed into Mg2 NiH4 upon hydrogen absorption.
During the hydrogenation process, both Mg2 Ni and Mg2 NiH4
acted as a "hydrogen pump" signiﬁcantly enhancing the diffu-
sion and transfer of hydrogen atoms. The MgH2 + Ni@Nb2 C
composite exhibited remarkable stability during hydrogen ab-
sorption and desorption cycles. However, due to the relatively
high  saturation  vapor  pressure  of  magnesium,  the  grains  of
MgH2  tended to enlarge after undergoing multiple cycles of
hydrogen  absorption  and  desorption.  This  phenomenon  re-
sults  in  a  slight  reduction  in  the  hydrogen  storage  capacity
of the composite materials following repeated cycling. These
ﬁndings  provide  signiﬁcant  support  for  advancing  efﬁcient
catalysts  for  hydrogen  storage  materials,  thereby  facilitating
progress  in  hydrogen  storage  technology  research.  This,  in
turn, is expected to expedite the practical implementation of
hydrogen fuel cell vehicles.
